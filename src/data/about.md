I'm Emmanuel, a software engineer focused on systems, reliability, and the technical foundations behind useful products.

Before I started writing software, I repaired mobile phones. Diagnosing a damaged device required patience and a clear process: observe the symptoms, isolate the failing component, understand why it failed, and test each repair carefully. That experience gave me an enduring respect for practical problem-solving and taught me that unfamiliar systems become manageable when I study them closely.

## Engineering work

I currently build mostly with **Rust** and **TypeScript**. Rust gives me precise control over performance, memory, and concurrency. TypeScript helps me move effectively across services, APIs, developer tools, and product-facing applications.

My role changes with the problem. On one project I may be thinking about data models, service boundaries, and failure recovery. On another, I may be designing an API, improving developer experience, investigating performance, or making a product decision that reduces unnecessary technical complexity. I value clear interfaces, explicit tradeoffs, useful documentation, and systems that remain understandable as they grow.

I have used Linux for years and am comfortable learning new tools, languages, and environments. I try to understand the underlying model rather than memorizing a framework. That approach makes it easier to transfer what I know between technologies and become productive in unfamiliar codebases.

## Systems and research

I spend a significant amount of time reading, researching, and writing about the systems beneath modern software. My main interests include:

- Distributed systems, coordination, replication, consistency, and fault tolerance
- Databases, storage engines, indexing, transactions, and query execution
- Compilers, language tooling, intermediate representations, and runtime behavior
- Operating systems, processes, memory, filesystems, and scheduling
- Networking, protocols, service communication, and observability
- Concurrency, parallelism, performance analysis, and resource efficiency

I am interested not only in how these systems work when everything is healthy, but also in how they behave under pressure. I like questions about partial failure, degraded networks, conflicting state, overloaded services, and the boundaries between strong guarantees and practical compromises.

My broader research interests include electric and autonomous vehicles, brain-computer interfaces, cryptocurrencies, pets, and animal behavior. These fields appeal to me because they combine technical systems with questions about people, environments, incentives, and trust.
